MultisymplecticIntegrators.SpaceTimeAnsatz — Type
SpaceTimeAnsatz

Abstract type of a space-time ansatz of one slab.

A slab is the space-time strip that one time step spans, [t_n, t_{n+1}] × xspan. The ansatz carries it in the normalised time coordinate t̂, where it is the rectangle [0, 1] × xspan.

An ansatz discretises the field u and its derivatives v = ∂ₜu and w = ∂ₓu on the slab, and gives the integrator the basis functions and the Jacobian from which it assembles the residual. It is not a method: a SpaceTimeMethod, such as SpaceTimeGalerkin, holds one.

GeometricBase.integrate — Method
integrate(problem::LagrangianPDEProblem, method::SpaceTimeMethod; on_failure = :warn, kwargs...)
integrate(integrator::GeometricIntegrator; on_failure = :warn)

Integrate problem with method and return the named tuple (; solution, traces, stats).

solution is the GeometricSolution on the output grid, and traces holds one entry per stored step: the top trace (u, v, w) of that step at the nodes of the space quadrature, as the method's top_trace gives it, or nothing where the method keeps none. The trace is needed at the quadrature nodes rather than the interior ones because the energy diagnostics integrate it there, at the accuracy of the Gauss rule.

stats is the named tuple (; iterations, residuals, initial_residuals, converged) of the nonlinear solves, with one entry per stored trace, as the method's solve_stats records them, or nothing where the method keeps no record; on_failure then has nothing to act on. A step has converged when its residual is at most f_abstol + f_reltol · r₀, with r₀ its initial residual and the tolerances of the integrator's own solver options; the term in r₀ counts only for a finite r₀, so a residual that is not finite never converges. A step that has not converged warns with on_failure = :warn, the default, and throws an ErrorException with on_failure = :error; both name the step. Any other value of on_failure throws an ArgumentError before the first step.

The steps are driven one at a time, because the trace of a step exists only while its cache does. Every call starts a new run: the integrator's cache is reset first, so its first slab takes the problem's initial data and the steps it records belong to this run alone. As in the ecosystem's integrate!, a step whose state holds a NaN ends the run with a warning; traces then ends at that step, and the solution holds the NaN there. A step whose nonlinear solve raises a NonlinearSolverException ends the run with a warning too; traces then ends at the step before it, and the solution is valid up to that step only. The keyword arguments other than on_failure go to the integrator's constructor, and to nothing else.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.CouplingData — Type
CouplingData

The coupling's block of the slab cache.

A coupling fixes how the free coefficients of a slab are laid out, adds terms to the residual, may transform the field, and may carry unknowns of its own between the coefficients and behind them. The block carries the coupling itself, in a field coupling, and what the coupling keeps between slabs — its datum, its unknowns, the tables its residual term evaluates and the scratch of that evaluation. It is built once, when the cache is built, and its type is a parameter of the cache. Every function of the coupling that runs within a step dispatches on the block.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.SpaceTimeGalerkin — Type
SpaceTimeGalerkin{A, C, G} <: SpaceTimeMethod

The space-time Galerkin slab integrator: an ansatz A, a coupling C of consecutive slabs and an initial guess G.

The ansatz is a SpaceTimeAnsatz, the coupling a SlabCoupling and the guess a SlabInitialGuess — Continuation or FitTo. A value of another role, a method in the place of the ansatz or a target in the place of the guess, is refused when the method is built.

One step of the integrator solves one slab. The unknown of a TensorBSplineAnsatz is its coefficient array, (Nt, Nx) per component, the unknown of a SymbolicAnsatz the parameter vector of its expression, and the unknown of a NetworkAnsatz the free part of its parameter vector. For the coefficient array, a coupling that fixes the bottom row of the time direction supplies it as a datum — the initial condition at the first step and the previous slab's top row after it — and the remaining Nt - 1 rows are determined by the interior weak Euler–Lagrange form of the slab, tested against the basis functions that vanish at the top of the slab. The v term of that form leaves one boundary term at the bottom of the slab, the momentum flux, which the coupling supplies as well; the top of the slab is free, so its flux is not imposed but measured, and handed on as the next slab's bottom flux.

The unknown count is the coupling's: the free rows of each space function and each component, plus the coupling's own unknowns behind them. A CoefficientRestriction fixes the bottom row, so it leaves (Nt - 1) * Nx * D free coefficients; a weak-multiplier coupling imposes the initial condition with multipliers of its own, so all Nt rows are free and the multipliers follow them. A symbolic ansatz has no rows, and neither has a network: their parameters are their free unknowns, and WeakInitialAndBoundaryConditions, the one coupling either takes, puts its multipliers after them. A step is a nonlinear system of total_free_coefficients(method, problem) equations, which the ecosystem's Newton solver solves, with a pivoted QR as its linear solve for a symbolic or a network ansatz. With linear_solver = true, for an ansatz whose step is affine in its unknowns and a linear density, one linear solve replaces Newton's iterations: the step's equations are the slab's weak-form rows — a network's interior weak form beside the coupling's weak initial and boundary rows — with the Jacobian of the whole system, and a solution is read from zero and then refined once with the same factorisation, two solves in all and no iterate. The factorisation is the solver's own linear method — the linear_solver_method of its configuration, which is SimpleSolvers.LapackPivotedQR for a network — taken at the run's first step and again at each step whose guess has moved the parameters the step holds fixed, because the Jacobian depends on them: for a :last_layer network those are the hidden parameters before its free output weights, of which the cache keeps a copy and compares it exactly. The Jacobian of such a step is a saddle point whose constraint rows reach only the free output weights of a :last_layer network, so the pivoted QR of a network solves it by its minimum-norm step, where a factorisation without rank detection raises on a zero pivot. In Float32 that step leaves a residual of order 1e-2 of the initial one — at most 2.4e-2 over three steps of LinearTransport at S = 20 and D = 2, where Float64 leaves 1.5e-14 — so the solver's default tolerances record such a step as not converged. The iterate count, the residual, the initial residual and the convergence of every step are recorded in the cache, and the trace (u, v, w) at the top of the slab, at the nodes of the space quadrature, is kept for the diagnostics.

The roles of the ansatz, the coupling and the guess, and the functions through which the core reaches each of them, are on the page The slab seam of the manual.

method = SpaceTimeGalerkin(
    TensorBSplineAnsatz(degree = 2, ncells = (1, 20), xspan = (0.0, 1.0)),
    CoefficientRestriction();
    initial_guess = Continuation(),
    linear_solver = false)

result = integrate(problem, method)

MultisymplecticIntegrators.SpaceTimeGalerkinCache — Type
SpaceTimeGalerkinCache{ST, AD, CD}

The cache of the slab integrator.

x is the nonlinear solver's vector: the free coefficients of the slab, laid out component-major with the time index fastest — the entry of free row a ∈ 1:Nf, space function j and component d is a + (j - 1) * Nf + (d - 1) * Nf * Nx, with Nf the coupling's free-row count — and then the coupling's own unknowns, in the layout of the coupling's block.

u, v and w hold the field and its derivatives at the points of the slab's quadrature, and du, dv and dw the density's derivatives there; trace_u, trace_v and trace_w hold the trace at the top of the last slab. iterations, residuals, initial_residuals and converged record the Newton solve of every step: its iterate count, its final residual ‖F(x)‖₂, its initial residual r₀ = ‖F(x₀)‖₂ and whether the step converged. The direct linear solve records its two solves, the first and the refinement step, as two iterates. steps counts the steps taken, which is what tells the coupling and the guess whether the slab is the run's first.

ansatz is the ansatz's block, of type AD, which holds what the ansatz keeps of a slab, and coupling the coupling's block, of type CD.

Clear the state a run leaves in the cache: the step counter, which is what tells the coupling and the guess whether the slab is the run's first, the counter of linear-solver factorisations, at zero of which the next step factorises again, and the per-step records of the Newton solve.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.transform_field! — Method
transform_field!(u, v, w, data, block, t̂, x, h, problem)

The coupling's transform of the field, applied to the ansatz's field u and its derivatives v = ∂ₜu and w = ∂ₓu at the points (t̂[q], x[r]) of the slab, as u[q, r, d], in place. t̂ is the normalised time of the slab, so the physical-time derivative of a term ℓ(t̂, x) is ∂_{t̂}ℓ / h. data is the coupling's block and block the ansatz's, so that a transform that is not a function of the point alone — one that projects the field, for instance — reads the parameters the step is solving for.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.interior_weak_form! — Method
interior_weak_form!(residual, ∂u, ∂v, ∂w, tabulation, h)

The interior weak Euler–Lagrange form of one slab, from the derivatives of the density at the quadrature points.

For a test function φ it reads

\[\int_0^1 \!\! \int_{x_{\min}}^{x_{\max}} \Bigl( h \, \frac{\partial L}{\partial u} \, \phi + \frac{\partial L}{\partial v} \, \partial_{\hat t} \phi + h \, \frac{\partial L}{\partial w} \, \partial_x \phi \Bigr) \, dx \, d\hat t ,\]

which is the variation of the action \int \int L \, dt \, dx with the derivatives left on the test function, dt = h \, d\hat t. The one factor of h that the v term does not carry is the 1 / h of ∂ₜu = ∂_{t̂}u / h, so the three terms are the three of δS with no factor dropped. The residual is that form for every basis function, in the coefficient layout of the slab.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.LevenbergMarquardt — Type
LevenbergMarquardt(; variable_projection = false) <: NonlinearSolverMethod

The Levenberg–Marquardt nonlinear solver of a fixed-boundary network slab.

The step is damped by a shift on the singular values of the Jacobian,

\[\delta(\mu) = -V \operatorname{diag}\bigl(\sigma_i / (\sigma_i^2 + \mu)\bigr) U^\top r,\]

from one decomposition of the Jacobian per accepted iterate, with the identity as the damping matrix. μ starts at 1e-6, is divided by 3 after an accepted step down to 1e-15, and multiplied by 4 after a rejected one. A trial point whose residual is not finite is rejected, and a guess whose residual is not finite ends the step at once with no iteration. The solve stops on the rule of step_converged with the integrator's own options, at max_iterations (300 by default), or once μ reaches 1e12. Its iteration count is the number of trial steps, accepted or rejected, and its residual is ‖F‖ of the whole system, so the slab's record path is the one every family uses.

With variable_projection = true the bubble's output weights are eliminated: an inner Newton solve brings their own residuals to the floor by the minimum-norm step of a truncated decomposition, and the outer solve is Levenberg–Marquardt on the Schur complement J_oo − J_oa J_aa⁺ J_ao. The residuals the cache records stay those of the whole system, so that a joint row and a projected row of the benchmark matrix compare one quantity.

A fixed-boundary slab's residual has a floor above the machine epsilon of its type under either variant, and the projected solve reaches a lower one than the joint solve; a row's convergence is recorded and not asserted, which is why both are offered.

integrate(problem, SpaceTimeGalerkin(FixedBoundaryNetwork(xspan = (0.0, 1.0)), FixedBoundary());
    solver = LevenbergMarquardt(variable_projection = true))

MultisymplecticIntegrators.energy_density — Function
energy_density(density, u, v, w, params = density_params(density))

The Hamiltonian density of a Lagrangian density, v ∂L/∂v − L.

For a density L(u, v, w) whose field has velocity v = ∂ₜu and slope w = ∂ₓu, the quantity conserved by the continuous problem is the integral of this density over space. It is read off the density's own API — the partial derivative with respect to v and the value — so it costs no new formula per problem, and it is the right notion for a density that couples the components of a vector field as well, where u, v and w are the vectors of the D components at one point and the product is their inner product.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.energy_error — Method

The relative deviation of a run's conserved energy from its value at the first stored step: one value per stored step, the first of which is zero.

traces is the second value integrate returns. The energy of a step is energy_density integrated over the space quadrature at the nodes the trace is stored on, which is why the rule is reached through the ansatz rather than through the problem: a LagrangianPDEProblem holds the space interval and not the rule on it. The deviation is relative to the first step and not to the initial time, because the trace of a step exists only at the top of the slab it solved, and no trace of t₀ is stored.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.noether_charge — Method

The rotation charge of every stored step, J = ∫ (u₁ v₂ − u₂ v₁) dx integrated by the method's own rule — at the nodes the trace of a slab method is stored on, and at the mean of each box's two nodes for the box scheme — and nothing for a density without the symmetry.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.BoxScheme — Type
BoxScheme() <: SpaceTimeMethod

The Preissmann box scheme: the baseline every other method of the package is compared against, advanced by integrate like any other method.

It discretises the first-order form

\[K z_t + L z_x = \nabla S(z), \qquad z = (u, v, w),\]

of a density λ(½|v|² − ½c²|w|² − V(u)) with λ > 0 and V a function of u alone, in which v = ∂ₜu and the third state is w = −c² ∂ₓu. The constant matrices are

\[K = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad L = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},\]

both skew-symmetric, and S(z) = V(u) + ½v² − ½w²/c². The scheme evaluates every row of that form at the centre of a box of the grid — the mean of its four corners for the value, the box's own differences for the derivatives — which is second order in the time step and in the space step, and which satisfies the discrete multisymplectic conservation law δₜω + δₓκ = 0 of the form, with ω = UᵀKV and κ = UᵀLV of two solutions of the scheme's linearisation, to round-off per box.

The class of densities

The example problems whose density is of that shape — Wave, VectorWave and SineGordon — are integrated; a density outside the class, such as LinearTransport's doubled field, raises an ArgumentError when the integrator is built. The class is decided on the partial derivatives the density supplies, never on the problem's type nor on its component count: SineGordon's density is in the class up to the constant factor c that its v term carries, and LinearTransport has two components and no such shape.

The resolution, and the state

The resolution is the problem's own: one box per timestep and xstep, and the state lives at the nodes of the output grid, (u, v, w) per component, so that the trace top_trace gives and the solution are on the grid the other methods write. The third state the scheme stores and solves for is the slope w = ∂ₓu, the form's third state divided by −c², and the trace carries it.

The system of one step is square: u at the interior nodes, v at every node but the first and w at every node are the unknowns, and the three rows of every box are the step's equations. u at both end nodes and v at the first node are the problem's boundary data; v needs one of them because the box rows see v only through sums over the box, so that an alternating mode of v is invisible to them and one datum fixes it. The step's initial guess is the level it starts from, continued, and the ecosystem's Newton solver — the one every method of the package uses — determines the step, so the solve's residuals and its convergence are the run's own record.

The energy

The energy that energy_error reports for a box scheme is the density's Hamiltonian at the mean of each box's two nodes, summed over the boxes with the boxes' widths as weights — the midpoint rule box_quadrature_weights gives.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.check_box_density — Method
check_box_density(problem) -> (λ, c²)

The coefficients λ and c² of the density's first-order form, or an ArgumentError naming the part of the class the density misses.

The class is λ(½|v|² − ½c²|w|² − V(u)) with λ > 0, c² > 0 and V a function of u alone, and it is decided by probing the partial derivatives the density itself supplies: ∂L/∂v must be λ v with one positive λ for every component, ∂L/∂w must be −λc² w with the same λ and one positive c², and ∂L/∂u must move with u alone.
