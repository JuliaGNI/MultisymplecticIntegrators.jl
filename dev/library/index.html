MultisymplecticIntegrators Library Functions

The problem types, and the slab integrator with its ansatz, couplings and initial guesses. The page The slab seam says how the parts of the slab integrator fit together, and the page Example problems and diagnostics holds the submodules of the four example problems and the diagnostics.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.AxisTabulation — Type
AxisTabulation{T}

The assembly tables of one axis of the tensor ansatz: at every point of the composite Gauss rule, the indices of the basis functions that are nonzero there and the values of those functions and of their first derivative, together with the nodes and the weights.

The tables are dense, q × width: a basis function of degree p is nonzero on p + 1 cells, so the support at one point is width functions wide (p + 1 away from an end, wider for a recombined basis, whose functions may reach further). An entry whose index is zero is padding and carries no term.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.CoefficientRestriction — Type
CoefficientRestriction() <: SlabCoupling

The coupling of two consecutive slabs by restriction: the top trace of the previous slab is restricted to the bottom constraints of the current one.

The bottom trace of a slab is its bottom row of coefficients, so the restriction is the row itself: the previous slab's top row is this slab's bottom datum, unchanged. The momentum flux goes with it. The v term of the interior weak form leaves one boundary term at the bottom of the slab, ∫ ∂L/∂v · φ dx at t̂ = 0, and the previous slab's top row of the weak form is exactly that load at its top — so the flux is not re-integrated from a trace but read off the row the previous slab already computed. At the first slab both come from the problem's initial condition.

The coupling adds no other term, because the interior weak form enforces everything else the slab needs.

source
MultisymplecticIntegrators.Continuation — Type
Continuation() <: SlabInitialGuess

The default initial guess: the previous slab's solution, carried into the new slab.

For the tensor B-spline ansatz under a CoefficientRestriction that is the previous slab's top coefficient row, repeated in every row: each row of the new slab starts where the previous slab ended. At the first slab there is nothing to carry, and the guess falls back to FitTo(InitialCondition()) with the ansatz's default fit method.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.CouplingData — Type
CouplingData

The coupling's block of the slab cache.

A coupling fixes how the free coefficients of a slab are laid out, adds terms to the residual, may transform the field, and may carry unknowns of its own between the coefficients and behind them. The block carries the coupling itself, in a field coupling, and what the coupling keeps between slabs — its datum, its unknowns, the tables its residual term evaluates and the scratch of that evaluation. It is built once, when the cache is built, and its type is a parameter of the cache. Every function of the coupling that runs within a step dispatches on the block.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.FitTo — Type
FitTo(target; method = nothing) <: SlabInitialGuess

Fit the ansatz to target on the slab, with the fit method method.

A target is evaluated on the slab — see InitialCondition, PreviousSlab, ExactSolution and TargetFunction — and the fit brings the ansatz to it. A method that is named is used as it is; an unnamed one resolves through default_fit(ansatz), so that an ansatz with a fit method of its own gets it.

target is a SlabTarget and method a SlabFitMethod; a value of another role, such as FitTo(Continuation()), has no method and is refused at construction.

A user-defined target is a subtype of SlabTarget with one method of

evaluate!(sample, target, cache, ansatz, problem, sol)

filling sample, of size (Qt, Qx, D), with the target's value at the quadrature points of the slab. The guess allocates sample and the fitted coefficients once per step; the slab cache holds no scratch of the guess.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.LagrangianDensity — Type
LagrangianDensity(L, u, v, w; params = NamedTuple())

The Lagrangian density L(u, v, w; params) of a field theory in one space dimension, where v = ∂ₜu and w = ∂ₓu, together with its three partial derivatives ∂u, ∂v and ∂w.

L, u, v and w are symbolic. Each of the four functions is built once by Symbolics.build_function(…; expression = Val(false)), so an evaluation is an ordinary Julia call and not a re-derivation of the expression. The names of params are the parameters of the density: every built function takes their values as further positional arguments.

Fields

  • L(u, v, w, p...): the density.
  • ∂u(u, v, w, p...), ∂v(u, v, w, p...), ∂w(u, v, w, p...): its partial derivatives.
  • names: the names of the parameters, in the order of the built functions' arguments.
  • params: the default parameter values.
  • linear: whether the partial derivatives are affine in (u, v, w).

Example

using Symbolics
@variables u v w
d = LagrangianDensity(v^2 / 2 - w^2 / 2, u, v, w; params = (c = 0.5,))
d(0.0, 1.0, 0.0, (c = 0.5,))   # the density, with the parameters named
d.∂v(0.0, 1.0, 0.0, 0.5)       # ∂L/∂v, with the values in argument order

MultisymplecticIntegrators.LagrangianPDEProblem — Type
LagrangianPDEProblem(density; D = 1, ics_function, bcs_function, solutions = nothing,
                     timespan, timestep, xspan, xstep, params = density.params)

A Lagrangian field theory in one space dimension on a space-time slab, with a generic element type.

density is a Lagrangian density: a callable L(u, v, w, params) with a derivatives method returning its three partial derivatives ∂L/∂u, ∂L/∂v and ∂L/∂w. A LagrangianDensity supplies both for one component at a time; a density that couples the components of the field, such as the vector density that VectorWave.vector_density builds, supplies them for the D components of one point at once. Such a density receives the D values of u, v and w at a point as an indexable collection: the couplings of the slab pass NTuple{D}s to derivatives, and energy_error passes Vectors, or scalars where D = 1, to derivatives and to the density itself. Write its methods for any collection of length D, not for AbstractVector alone; derivatives returns the named tuple (u = …, v = …, w = …) of the D values of each partial derivative. The problem's parameters are the density's own params field where it has one, and the params keyword otherwise.

ics_function(x, params) returns the named tuple (u = …, v = …, w = …) of the initial data on the vector x of grid points, each entry a length(x) × D matrix whose column d holds component d. bcs_function(t, params) returns the side data of the slab at the one time t: left and right, each (u = …, v = …, w = …) of D vectors, entry d component d's value at that end. solutions is nothing where the problem has no exact solution, and otherwise the named tuple (u = …, v = …, w = …) of (t, x, params) functions that return the D components' values at a point and the component-major vector of length D * length(x) at a grid.

timespan, timestep, xspan and xstep are converted to the element type T of timespan. The functions of the problem take its parameters, never a module-level default.

problem = LagrangianPDEProblem(density;
    ics_function = (x, p) -> (u = …, v = …, w = …),
    bcs_function = (t, p) -> …,
    timespan = (0.0, 1.0), timestep = 0.1, xspan = (0.0, 1.0), xstep = 0.01)

MultisymplecticIntegrators.PreviousSlab — Type
PreviousSlab() <: SlabTarget

The previous slab's solution, extrapolated in time beyond the top of that slab.

For the B-spline ansatz the extrapolation is the polynomial piece of the slab's top time cell, expanded about its top and evaluated at the displacement t̂ beyond it: a spline is a polynomial on each cell, so that piece extends the solution without inventing anything. At the first slab, where there is no previous solution, the target falls back to InitialCondition().

MultisymplecticIntegrators.Projection — Type
Projection() <: SlabFitMethod

The fit method of an ansatz that is linear in its coefficients: the L² projection of the target onto the slab's ansatz space, on the quadrature of the slab.

The projection solves the mass-matrix system of the ansatz, which SimpleSplines factorises once per quadrature rather than once per step. An ansatz whose coefficients enter a nonlinearity has no such system, and a Projection() on one is refused at construction — see SpaceTimeGalerkin.

A user-defined fit method is a subtype of SlabFitMethod, and places the coefficients of coeffs, of size (Nt, Nx, D), from the sampled target, in one method of

fit!(coeffs, method, ansatz, sample, cache)

cache is the SpaceTimeGalerkinCache of the slab, which a fit may read; it holds no scratch of the fit.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.SpaceTimeAnsatz — Type
SpaceTimeAnsatz

Abstract type of a space-time ansatz of one slab.

A slab is the space-time strip that one time step spans, [t_n, t_{n+1}] × xspan. The ansatz carries it in the normalised time coordinate t̂, where it is the rectangle [0, 1] × xspan.

An ansatz discretises the field u and its derivatives v = ∂ₜu and w = ∂ₓu on the slab, and gives the integrator the basis functions and the Jacobian from which it assembles the residual. It is not a method: a SpaceTimeMethod, such as SpaceTimeGalerkin, holds one.

source
MultisymplecticIntegrators.SpaceTimeGalerkin — Type
SpaceTimeGalerkin{A, C, G} <: SpaceTimeMethod

The space-time Galerkin slab integrator: an ansatz A, a coupling C of consecutive slabs and an initial guess G.

The ansatz is a SpaceTimeAnsatz, the coupling a SlabCoupling and the guess a SlabInitialGuess — Continuation or FitTo. A value of another role, a method in the place of the ansatz or a target in the place of the guess, is refused when the method is built.

One step of the integrator solves one slab. The unknown is the coefficient array of the ansatz, (Nt, Nx) per component. A coupling that fixes the bottom row of the time direction supplies it as a datum — the initial condition at the first step and the previous slab's top row after it — and the remaining Nt - 1 rows are determined by the interior weak Euler–Lagrange form of the slab, tested against the basis functions that vanish at the top of the slab. The v term of that form leaves one boundary term at the bottom of the slab, the momentum flux, which the coupling supplies as well; the top of the slab is free, so its flux is not imposed but measured, and handed on as the next slab's bottom flux.

The unknown count is the coupling's: the free rows of each space function and each component, plus the coupling's own unknowns behind them. A CoefficientRestriction fixes the bottom row, so it leaves (Nt - 1) * Nx * D free coefficients; a weak-multiplier coupling imposes the initial condition with multipliers of its own, so all Nt rows are free and the multipliers follow them. A step is a nonlinear system of total_free_coefficients(method, problem) equations, which the ecosystem's Newton solver solves. With linear_solver = true, for an ansatz linear in its coefficients and a linear density, the system is affine with the same Jacobian on every slab: the first step factorises it once, and every step solves with that factorisation directly and then takes one step of iterative refinement with it, two solves in all. The iterate count, the residual, the initial residual and the convergence of every step are recorded in the cache, and the trace (u, v, w) at the top of the slab, at the nodes of the space quadrature, is kept for the diagnostics.

The roles of the ansatz, the coupling and the guess, and the functions through which the core reaches each of them, are on the page The slab seam of the manual.

method = SpaceTimeGalerkin(
    TensorBSplineAnsatz(degree = 2, ncells = (1, 20), xspan = (0.0, 1.0)),
    CoefficientRestriction();
    initial_guess = Continuation(),
    linear_solver = false)

result = integrate(problem, method)

MultisymplecticIntegrators.SpaceTimeGalerkinCache — Type
SpaceTimeGalerkinCache{ST, AD, CD}

The cache of the slab integrator.

x is the nonlinear solver's vector: the free coefficients of the slab, laid out component-major with the time index fastest — the entry of free row a ∈ 1:Nf, space function j and component d is a + (j - 1) * Nf + (d - 1) * Nf * Nx, with Nf the coupling's free-row count — and then the coupling's own unknowns, in the layout of the coupling's block.

u, v and w hold the field and its derivatives at the points of the slab's quadrature, and du, dv and dw the density's derivatives there; trace_u, trace_v and trace_w hold the trace at the top of the last slab. iterations, residuals, initial_residuals and converged record the Newton solve of every step: its iterate count, its final residual ‖F(x)‖₂, its initial residual r₀ = ‖F(x₀)‖₂ and whether the step converged. The direct linear solve records its two solves, the first and the refinement step, as two iterates. steps counts the steps taken, which is what tells the coupling and the guess whether the slab is the run's first.

ansatz is the ansatz's block, of type AD, which holds what the ansatz keeps of a slab, and coupling the coupling's block, of type CD.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.TensorBSplineAnsatz — Type
TensorBSplineAnsatz(; degree, ncells, xspan, x_boundary = Dirichlet(), n = nothing)

A tensor B-spline ansatz of one space-time slab, on SimpleSplines.

The slab is the rectangle [0, 1] × xspan in the normalised time coordinate t̂ and the physical space coordinate x. The ansatz approximates the field by

\[u_h(t̂, x) = \sum_{i,j} \hat{u}_{ij} \, B_i(t̂) \, B_j(x),\]

with a clamped B-spline basis of degree degree[1] on ncells[1] uniform cells of [0, 1] and a B-spline basis of degree degree[2] on ncells[2] uniform cells of xspan. degree and ncells are given per direction, as a number for both or as a (time, space) tuple, so that the two directions may differ in degree and in cell count.

x_boundary is the boundary condition the space basis is built with: Dirichlet() recombines the basis at both ends of xspan, so that every function vanishes there, and Free() keeps the natural condition. The ansatz carries a homogeneous Dirichlet value in x only; an inhomogeneous value is a coupling's business.

n is the number of Gauss–Legendre nodes per cell in each direction, again a number or a (time, space) tuple; the default integrates the products of the basis functions and of their first derivatives exactly, which is p + 1 nodes for degree p, the count the products of the basis functions need.

Both degrees are at least 1. The slab core reads the space gradient ∂ₓu from the same space basis, and a piecewise-constant degree-0 basis carries none, so a lower degree raises ArgumentError.

The ansatz counts the degree, which is what SimpleSplines takes; a basis of order k is one of degree k - 1.

ansatz = TensorBSplineAnsatz(degree = 2, ncells = (1, 20), xspan = (0.0, 1.0))

MultisymplecticIntegrators.WeakInitialAndBoundaryConditions — Type
WeakInitialAndBoundaryConditions(; multiplier_basis = :bspline) <: SlabCoupling

The initial condition and the space boundary conditions of a slab, imposed weakly.

The initial condition is imposed as WeakInitialCondition imposes it. The multipliers at x = a and at x = b are unknowns of the step as well, and their rows are

\[\int \bigl( g_a(t) - u(t, a) \bigr) \, \chi_p(t) \, dt , \qquad \int \bigl( g_b(t) - u(t, b) \bigr) \, \chi_p(t) \, dt ,\]

where g_a and g_b are the problem's side traces, evaluated at each node of the time rule, so that inhomogeneous data is imposed as it stands. The multiplier basis is the ansatz's own trace basis, or the nodal basis of that same set of functions, as multiplier_basis (alias basis) names it, so that a slab with Nt time functions and Nx space functions has Nx - 2 multipliers at the bottom surface, without its two corner functions, and Nt at each end of xspan.

The boundary rows carry an unknown, so the ansatz must take x_boundary = Free(); every other value throws an ArgumentError naming the condition the coupling needs and the one it got. A Dirichlet() basis in particular makes the trace vanish at each end of xspan, which would leave the rows empty.

method = SpaceTimeGalerkin(
    TensorBSplineAnsatz(degree = 2, ncells = (2, 20), xspan = (0, 1),
        x_boundary = Free()),
    WeakInitialAndBoundaryConditions())

MultisymplecticIntegrators.WeakInitialCondition — Type
WeakInitialCondition() <: SlabCoupling
WeakInitialCondition(; multiplier_basis = :bspline) <: SlabCoupling

The initial condition of a slab, imposed weakly.

The bottom row of the coefficient array is free, so all Nt rows of the slab are unknowns, and the slab is closed by one row per multiplier function ψ_p,

\[\int \bigl( u_0(x) - u(0, x) \bigr) \, \psi_p(x) \, dx ,\]

where u₀ is the problem's initial condition on the first slab and the previous slab's top trace on every later one. The multiplier at the bottom surface enters the momentum balance as the momentum that enters the slab: on the first slab, the quadrature-weighted L² projection of ∂L/∂v(u₀, v₀, w₀) at the nodes of the space rule onto the multiplier functions, and the previous slab's top multiplier afterwards.

multiplier_basis names the basis of ψ_p: :bspline, the ansatz's own trace basis, or :lagrange, the nodal basis of that same set of functions. basis is an alias of the keyword.

The coupling imposes no space boundary condition, so the ends of xspan are left to the space basis: the ansatz must take x_boundary = Dirichlet(), and every other value throws an ArgumentError that names the condition the coupling needs and the one it got. A Free() basis leaves the ends unconstrained, and the remaining conditions constrain them in a way the interior weak form does not carry.

method = SpaceTimeGalerkin(
    TensorBSplineAnsatz(degree = 2, ncells = (2, 20), xspan = (0, 1)),
    WeakInitialCondition())

GeometricBase.integrate — Method
integrate(problem::LagrangianPDEProblem, method::SpaceTimeMethod; on_failure = :warn, kwargs...)
integrate(integrator::GeometricIntegrator; on_failure = :warn)

Integrate problem with method and return the named tuple (; solution, traces, stats).

solution is the GeometricSolution on the output grid, and traces holds one entry per stored step: the top trace (u, v, w) of that step at the nodes of the space quadrature, as the method's top_trace gives it, or nothing where the method keeps none. The trace is needed at the quadrature nodes rather than the interior ones because the energy diagnostics integrate it there, at the accuracy of the Gauss rule.

stats is the named tuple (; iterations, residuals, initial_residuals, converged) of the nonlinear solves, with one entry per stored trace, as the method's solve_stats records them, or nothing where the method keeps no record; on_failure then has nothing to act on. A step has converged when its residual is at most f_abstol + f_reltol · r₀, with r₀ its initial residual and the tolerances of the integrator's own solver options; the term in r₀ counts only for a finite r₀, so a residual that is not finite never converges. A step that has not converged warns with on_failure = :warn, the default, and throws an ErrorException with on_failure = :error; both name the step. Any other value of on_failure throws an ArgumentError before the first step.

The steps are driven one at a time, because the trace of a step exists only while its cache does. Every call starts a new run: the integrator's cache is reset first, so its first slab takes the problem's initial data and the steps it records belong to this run alone. As in the ecosystem's integrate!, a step whose state holds a NaN ends the run with a warning; traces then ends at that step, and the solution holds the NaN there. A step whose nonlinear solve raises a NonlinearSolverException ends the run with a warning too; traces then ends at the step before it, and the solution is valid up to that step only. The keyword arguments other than on_failure go to the integrator's constructor, and to nothing else.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.energy_density — Function
energy_density(density, u, v, w, params = density_params(density))

The Hamiltonian density of a Lagrangian density, v ∂L/∂v − L.

For a density L(u, v, w) whose field has velocity v = ∂ₜu and slope w = ∂ₓu, the quantity conserved by the continuous problem is the integral of this density over space. It is read off the density's own API — the partial derivative with respect to v and the value — so it costs no new formula per problem, and it is the right notion for a density that couples the components of a vector field as well, where u, v and w are the vectors of the D components at one point and the product is their inner product.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.energy_error — Method
energy_error(problem, method, traces)
energy_error(problem, ansatz, traces)

The relative deviation of a run's conserved energy from its value at the first stored step: one value per stored step, the first of which is zero.

traces is the second value integrate returns. The energy of a step is energy_density integrated over the space quadrature at the nodes the trace is stored on, which is why the rule is reached through the ansatz rather than through the problem: a LagrangianPDEProblem holds the space interval and not the rule on it. The deviation is relative to the first step and not to the initial time, because the trace of a step exists only at the top of the slab it solved, and no trace of t₀ is stored.

A run whose first energy vanishes — an all-zero trace, for one — has no relative scale, and its deviation is the absolute one.

A run that stored no trace raises an ArgumentError: the energy of a step is a function of its trace, and no other value of the run carries the velocity and the slope at the quadrature nodes. A trace whose u, v and w hold different numbers of entries, or whose length is not a whole number of the problem's components, raises one as well rather than being read at the nodes it happens to hold.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.interior_weak_form! — Method
interior_weak_form!(residual, ∂u, ∂v, ∂w, tabulation, h)

The interior weak Euler–Lagrange form of one slab, from the derivatives of the density at the quadrature points.

For a test function φ it reads

\[\int_0^1 \!\! \int_{x_{\min}}^{x_{\max}} \Bigl( h \, \frac{\partial L}{\partial u} \, \phi + \frac{\partial L}{\partial v} \, \partial_{\hat t} \phi + h \, \frac{\partial L}{\partial w} \, \partial_x \phi \Bigr) \, dx \, d\hat t ,\]

which is the variation of the action \int \int L \, dt \, dx with the derivatives left on the test function, dt = h \, d\hat t. The one factor of h that the v term does not carry is the 1 / h of ∂ₜu = ∂_{t̂}u / h, so the three terms are the three of δS with no factor dropped. The residual is that form for every basis function, in the coefficient layout of the slab.
