The slab seam

A SpaceTimeGalerkin method holds three parts: an ansatz, a coupling and an initial guess. The core of the integrator — residual!, update! and integrate_step! — knows none of them by type. It reaches each part through the functions on this page, and each part keeps its own data in a block of the slab cache.

The cache

The SpaceTimeGalerkinCache holds what every method fills, whatever its parts:

fieldwhat it holdsfilled by
xthe solver's vector of the slabthe guess, then the Newton solver
u, v, wthe field and its derivatives at the points of the slab's quadraturethe ansatz, then the coupling's transform
du, dv, dwthe density's derivatives at those pointsthe core
trace_u, trace_v, trace_wthe field at the top of the last slab, at the nodes of the space quadraturethe ansatz, then the coupling's transform
iterations, residuals, initial_residuals, convergedthe record of every step's Newton solvethe core
stepsthe number of steps taken in the runthe core
ansatzthe ansatz's blockthe ansatz
couplingthe coupling's blockthe coupling

The guess keeps nothing in the cache: it allocates its scratch once per step.

The ansatz

The ansatz discretises the field on one slab, and owns the quadrature of the slab. Its block is built by ansatz_data, whose type ansatz_data_type gives without building it. For a TensorBSplineAnsatz the block is a TensorBSplineSlab: the coefficient array, its weak form, the top row of the last slab, the tables of the slab's quadrature, and the space basis at the output grid and at the trace nodes. For a SymbolicAnsatz the block is a SymbolicSlab: the parameter vector, its weak form, the field and its parameter Jacobians at the nodes and on the four edges of the slab, the tables of the slab's quadrature, and the trace basis' space basis at the output grid and at the trace nodes. For a NetworkAnsatz the block is a NetworkSlab: the parameter vector, its weak form, the field and its parameter derivative at the nodes of the ansatz's own rule and on the four edges of the slab, the tables of that rule and of the trace basis', and the scratch of one point evaluation.

The core calls these functions of the ansatz, each on its block:

  • quadrature_points: the time and the space nodes of the slab's quadrature;
  • slab_field!: the field and its derivatives at those nodes, from the solver's vector and the coupling's fixed bottom row;
  • slab_weak_form! and free_residual!: the weak form of the density's derivatives, and its rows of the free coefficients in the solver's layout;
  • keep_top!: keep the top row of the solved slab, which a symbolic ansatz, with no rows, does not have;
  • top_values!, with output_evaluation, trace_evaluation and evaluation_points: the field at the top of the slab, at the output grid and at the trace nodes, from one evaluation;
  • check_ansatz: the refusal of an ansatz that the problem cannot support;
  • copy_ansatz_data!: what the block keeps outside the solver's vector, from the cache of the problem's element type into the cache the solver works in; the default copies nothing.

The solver's vector of a slab holds the ansatz's unknowns and then the coupling's. nunknowns counts the ansatz's: for a tensor B-spline ansatz the free rows of its ntime_functions, times nspace_functions, times the components, and for a network the parameters its mode frees. slab_unknowns adds the coupling's unknowns. A symbolic ansatz counts its parameters, and total_free_coefficients gives the size for every ansatz.

The coupling

The coupling joins consecutive slabs: it fixes the datum of a slab, lays out the free rows of the solver's vector, adds its terms and its own equations to the residual, and may transform the field. Its block is a CouplingData that carries the coupling itself, built by coupling_data, with its type from coupling_data_type. A coupling that keeps nothing gets a NoCouplingData; CoefficientRestriction keeps a RestrictionData, its bottom row and its momentum load; the weak couplings keep a MultiplierData, their multipliers and the tables of their trace basis.

Before a block exists, the core asks the coupling itself:

  • free_rows: the free time rows, declared by every coupling, with no default;
  • coupling_unknowns: the number of the coupling's own unknowns;
  • check_coupling: the refusal of an ansatz that the coupling cannot support;
  • coupling_internal: the coupling's entries of the solution step's internal state.

Within a step, every function of the coupling dispatches on its block:

  • slab_bottom!: fix the datum of the slab;
  • bottom_datum: the bottom row the coupling fixes, or nothing;
  • transform_field!: the coupling's transform of the field, wherever the core evaluates the field — in the residual, the trace and the solution; the default leaves the field as it is;
  • coupling_term!: the coupling's terms of the residual, with no default, so that a coupling without a term declares its no-op;
  • coupling_guess!: the guess of the coupling's own unknowns;
  • coupling_update!: what the coupling reads out of the solved slab and hands on;
  • copy_coupling_data!: the datum, from the cache of the problem's element type into the cache the solver works in.

The guess

The initial guess fills the solver's vector before each solve, through initial_guess!: Continuation repeats the previous slab's top row, or, for a symbolic ansatz, takes the ansatz's start vector on the first slab and the previous slab's parameters after it, and FitTo evaluates a SlabTarget on the slab with evaluate! and fits the ansatz to it with a SlabFitMethod through fit!, whose contract Projection states. The coupling then guesses its own unknowns.

A step

integrate_step! runs one slab: slab_bottom!, the guess, the Newton solve, the record of the solve, and update!, which evaluates the solved slab, writes the solution and the trace, and calls coupling_update!. integrate drives the steps and returns the solution, the traces and the solve_stats of the run.

The action form (U17)

The interior weak form of a slab is the gradient of its discrete action with respect to the parameters ĉ of the ansatz, the whole coefficient array for the tensor B-spline ansatz and the parameter vector for the symbolic ansatz, and the residual keeps the rows of it that the free rows carry. The action is the sum over the nodes of the slab's quadrature of the weighted density L(u, v, w), where u, v and w are the field and its derivatives that ĉ gives at the nodes. By the chain rule, the gradient is the pullback of the density's derivatives ∂u, ∂v, ∂w at the nodes through the map from ĉ to the field there. The core already evaluates the residual in that order, one seam function per factor:

  1. slab_field! applies the field map: it builds ĉ from the solver's vector and the coupling's bottom row, and gives the field at the nodes;
  2. transform_field! applies the coupling's transform; a lift ℓ(t̂, x) that does not depend on ĉ changes the point at which the density is evaluated and not the pullback;
  3. the core evaluates ∂u, ∂v and ∂w of the density at the nodes;
  4. slab_weak_form! pulls them back through the field map, with the quadrature weights;
  5. free_residual! writes the rows of the free coefficients in the solver's layout.

For the tensor B-spline ansatz the field map is linear in ĉ, so its pullback is the product with the tabulated basis, which is the weak form in src/core/weak_form.jl. An ansatz whose field map is not linear, such as a network, implements slab_field! as its map and slab_weak_form! as the pullback of ∂u, ∂v and ∂w through that map by automatic differentiation, with free_residual! on its own block. The symbolic ansatz is such an ansatz, and its pullback reads the parameter Jacobians that it differentiates symbolically when it is built. A coupling may compute coupling_term! as the gradient, by automatic differentiation, of its own action terms: the function receives the solver's vector x and the coupling's block, which is all those terms read. Neither the core nor the existing couplings change for either.

The variant that the seam carries is the pullback of ∂L at the nodes: the core evaluates the density's derivatives, and the ansatz differentiates only its field map. The seam does not differentiate L inside the ansatz's block, so an ansatz never sees the density, and the density keeps the derivatives that its problem defines.
