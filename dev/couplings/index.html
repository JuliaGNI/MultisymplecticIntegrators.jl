Couplings and initial guesses

The couplings that close a slab's system, and the guess that seeds its solve: a target, the five targets this package ships, and the fits that bring an ansatz to one of them.

The hard-constraint lifting

HardConstraintLifting is the coupling of the network family that imposes a slab's data on the field itself. The field of the slab is P[N] + ℓ: the transfinite projection of the ansatz's field that vanishes at the two ends of xspan and at the bottom of the slab, plus the transfinite interpolant ℓ of the slab's data — the problem's boundary function at the two ends, and the initial condition's bottom on the first slab and the previous slab's top afterwards. The initial and boundary conditions are therefore met by construction and the interior weak form alone is the residual.

The slab carries no momentum between steps: the coupling adds no surface term and no load at the bottom of the slab, and coupling_unknowns is zero, so the solver's vector is the ansatz's own. That is this coupling's formulation, kept on purpose; a formulation that carries the incoming momentum at the bottom of each slab is not this one.

A NetworkAnsatz and a TensorBSplineAnsatz are both accepted, and an ansatz of any other kind is refused when the integrator is built. For a network the transform projects the field's derivative with respect to every free parameter together with the field, so the residual is the gradient of the slab's discrete action on the free parameters; for a coefficient ansatz there is no parameter derivative and the transform places the data in the field alone, which needs x_boundary = Dirichlet(): the transform reads the field of the slab's two ends at the time nodes of the residual, so a basis that is free there is refused.

SimpleSplines counts the degree of a basis and not its order, so a trace basis of order 4 is built with degree = 3.

The fits

PINNTraining descends a network's free parameters on its misfit against a sampled target, with a fixed step and no optimiser package: the sample's points are drawn once per fit from a copy of the method's generator, so two fits that share one method object draw the same points, each pass over them takes one step per batch of batch points, and the gradient is the parameter derivative the ansatz's block already carries.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.CoefficientRestriction — Type
CoefficientRestriction() <: SlabCoupling

The coupling of two consecutive slabs by restriction: the top trace of the previous slab is restricted to the bottom constraints of the current one.

The bottom trace of a slab is its bottom row of coefficients, so the restriction is the row itself: the previous slab's top row is this slab's bottom datum, unchanged. The momentum flux goes with it. The v term of the interior weak form leaves one boundary term at the bottom of the slab, ∫ ∂L/∂v · φ dx at t̂ = 0, and the previous slab's top row of the weak form is exactly that load at its top — so the flux is not re-integrated from a trace but read off the row the previous slab already computed. At the first slab both come from the problem's initial condition.

The coupling adds no other term, because the interior weak form enforces everything else the slab needs.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.WeakInitialAndBoundaryConditions — Type
WeakInitialAndBoundaryConditions(; multiplier_basis = :bspline) <: SlabCoupling

The initial condition and the space boundary conditions of a slab, imposed weakly.

The initial condition is imposed as WeakInitialCondition imposes it. The multipliers at x = a and at x = b are unknowns of the step as well, and their rows are

\[\int \bigl( g_a(t) - u(t, a) \bigr) \, \chi_p(t) \, dt , \qquad \int \bigl( g_b(t) - u(t, b) \bigr) \, \chi_p(t) \, dt ,\]

where g_a and g_b are the problem's side traces, evaluated at each node of the time rule, so that inhomogeneous data is imposed as it stands. The multiplier basis is the ansatz's own trace basis, or the nodal basis of that same set of functions, as multiplier_basis (alias basis) names it, so that a slab with Nt time functions and Nx space functions has Nx - 2 multipliers at the bottom surface, without its two corner functions, and Nt at each end of xspan.

The boundary rows carry an unknown, so the ansatz must take x_boundary = Free(); every other value throws an ArgumentError naming the condition the coupling needs and the one it got. A Dirichlet() basis in particular makes the trace vanish at each end of xspan, which would leave the rows empty.

method = SpaceTimeGalerkin(
    TensorBSplineAnsatz(degree = 2, ncells = (2, 20), xspan = (0, 1),
        x_boundary = Free()),
    WeakInitialAndBoundaryConditions())

MultisymplecticIntegrators.WeakInitialCondition — Type
WeakInitialCondition() <: SlabCoupling
WeakInitialCondition(; multiplier_basis = :bspline) <: SlabCoupling

The initial condition of a slab, imposed weakly.

The bottom row of the coefficient array is free, so all Nt rows of the slab are unknowns, and the slab is closed by one row per multiplier function ψ_p,

\[\int \bigl( u_0(x) - u(0, x) \bigr) \, \psi_p(x) \, dx ,\]

where u₀ is the problem's initial condition on the first slab and the previous slab's top trace on every later one. The multiplier at the bottom surface enters the momentum balance as the momentum that enters the slab: on the first slab, the quadrature-weighted L² projection of ∂L/∂v(u₀, v₀, w₀) at the nodes of the space rule onto the multiplier functions, and the previous slab's top multiplier afterwards.

multiplier_basis names the basis of ψ_p: :bspline, the ansatz's own trace basis, or :lagrange, the nodal basis of that same set of functions. basis is an alias of the keyword.

The coupling imposes no space boundary condition, so the ends of xspan are left to the space basis: the ansatz must take x_boundary = Dirichlet(), and every other value throws an ArgumentError that names the condition the coupling needs and the one it got. A Free() basis leaves the ends unconstrained, and the remaining conditions constrain them in a way the interior weak form does not carry.

method = SpaceTimeGalerkin(
    TensorBSplineAnsatz(degree = 2, ncells = (2, 20), xspan = (0, 1)),
    WeakInitialCondition())

MultisymplecticIntegrators.BubbleGreedy — Type
BubbleGreedy(; nα = 13, nβ = 13, nγ = 13, α_range = (-8.0, 8.0), β_range = (-8.0, 8.0),
              γ_range = (-6.0, 6.0), iterations = 60)

The fit that starts a FixedBoundaryNetwork slab: the level's parameters by Levenberg–Marquardt from the datum to the target at the top of the slab, and then the bubble by a greedy selection of St atoms out of a dictionary of tanh(α t̂ + β ŝ + γ) units.

The dictionary is nα × nβ × nγ atoms on the three ranges, less the atoms at α = β = 0, which no (θ, s, ρ) writes; both forms of the bubble therefore start from the same atoms, and the units are read in the form's own coordinates. The selection takes, at every step, the atom whose correlation with the target's remainder is largest in absolute value over the atoms and the components, as the greedy fit of a network does, and its output weights are the least-squares solution on the atoms taken so far.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.FixedBoundary — Type
FixedBoundary() <: SlabCoupling

The coupling of the fixed-boundary network: it carries the datum of a slab — the previous level's parameters φ₀ and the momentum p₀ that level received — and hands the momentum of the solved level on to the next slab. It has no multiplier and no equation of its own: the step's equations are the action's stationarity alone.

A FixedBoundary takes a FixedBoundaryNetwork and nothing else, and that ansatz takes this coupling and nothing else; either mismatch raises ArgumentError when the integrator is built.

source
MultisymplecticIntegrators.FixedBoundaryData — Type
FixedBoundaryData{ST} <: CouplingData

The block of a FixedBoundary in the slab cache.

phi and momentum are the datum's pair (φ₀, p₀), the previous level's parameters and the covector on the free ones. ga and gb hold the two ends' side data, one row per time of the slab: the slab's start time in row 1, its nodes in rows 2:(qt + 1), and its top time in row qt + 2; dga and dgb hold the same rows' derivatives in physical time. start is the slab's start time.

phi_next is the solved level's parameters and p_next the momentum the step hands on; the next slab's datum is filled from these two copies. v_top is the solved slab's velocity at its top time, on the predictor's grid, which a later slab's predictor starts from. All three are taken when the step's solution is written, so that no buffer a later residual or a later guess rewrites can move them.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.FixedBoundaryFit — Type
FixedBoundaryFit{ST}

The workspace of the fit that starts a fixed-boundary slab: the dictionary its method builds, that dictionary tabulated at the slab's nodes and weighted by √(ωₜωₓ), the buffers of the greedy selection over the atoms and the components, and the output weights it leaves.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.Predictor — Type
Predictor() <: SlabTarget

A cheap predictor of the slab, as the target of a FitTo: Störmer–Verlet on a uniform grid of the span, from the bottom level's field and either the initial data's velocity or the previous slab's top velocity, with the side data imposed at every substep. The guess fits the level to the predictor's top and the bubble to what the predictor has beyond the interpolation of the two levels.

It is a target of a FixedBoundaryNetwork alone, and its acceleration reads the density's velocity derivative as one constant m times the velocity — which holds for the wave equation, for sine-Gordon and for a generated density, and fails for a density whose momentum is not of that form, such as a doubled linear transport. Either mismatch raises ArgumentError when the integrator is built.

GeometricIntegratorsBase.default_options — Method
default_options(method, problem)

The family's solver options: a step converges on the rule of step_converged at f_abstol = 0 and the element type's own relative tolerance, and a solve is capped at 300 iterations. They are the defaults of both the family's solver and the Newton() a caller may name instead, so that an affine control and a dead branch are measured under one rule.

The tolerance is handed on as nothing, because GeometricIntegrator merges these defaults into the caller's keywords before any solver is built: the family's initsolver resolves it from fixed_boundary_f_reltol once the caller's own keywords are known, so that a caller who names an f_reltol at an element type the family has not measured is not refused for it.

GeometricIntegratorsBase.initsolver — Method
initsolver(solvermethod::Newton, method, caches; kwargs...)

A fixed-boundary slab's rows are the derivative of a discrete action, so its Jacobian is a saddle point whose rank is below its size: Newton() takes the minimum-norm step of a complete orthogonal factorization, as the multiplier network does.

The factorization's rank cut is fixed_boundary_qr_cut, which the default cut √eps(T) would leave the step's residual stalled above. A caller's own linear_solver_method still overrides it.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.fixed_boundary_f_reltol — Method
fixed_boundary_f_reltol(::Type{T})

The relative tolerance of a fixed-boundary step's convergence rule, at element type T.

Float64 is 1e-8. On Wave at h = 0.05 the dead branch's twenty steps reach a worst ratio of 2.96e-10 under this rule, with the rank cut of the pivoted QR at 1e-12; the tolerance is not what limits that run, since the factorization's own cut leaves one step at 3.0e-8. Float32 is 3.0f-2, the value measured on the family's affine control at that element type, whose worst ratio is 1.365e-2; it is never the Float64 value converted. Any other element type is refused rather than given the floor of its own round-off: the rule is a measurement on the family's control and not a formula, so a run at another type names the tolerance it is solved at as an option of its own, and a run that names none is told the type it asked for.

source
MultisymplecticIntegrators.fixed_boundary_qr_cut — Method
fixed_boundary_qr_cut(::Type{T})

The relative rank cut of the pivoted QR a Newton() step takes, at element type T.

Float64 is 1e-12. The factorization's own default √eps(T) = 1.49e-8 drops two singular directions of a slab's Jacobian in which the residual stalls, so the cut is the floor the step reaches rather than the factorization's guess. Another element type takes 10 eps(T), the same floor in its own round-off: a cut below it would keep directions that the factorization at that precision cannot resolve.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.HardConstraintLifting — Type
HardConstraintLifting() <: SlabCoupling

The coupling of the hard-constraint formulation: the field of the slab is the ansatz's field projected onto the slab's data, plus the data.

The projection is the transfinite one, which vanishes at the two ends of xspan and at the bottom of the slab,

\[P[N] = N - T_1[N] + T_2[N],\quad T_1[N] = \psi_L(x) N(\hat t, a) + \psi_R(x) N(\hat t, b) + (1 - \hat t) N(0, x),\quad T_2[N] = (1 - \hat t) \left(\psi_L(x) N(0, a) + \psi_R(x) N(0, b)\right),\]

with ψ_L(x) = (b - x)/(b - a) and ψ_R(x) = (x - a)/(b - a), and the lift is its counterpart on the slab's data,

\[\ell = \psi_L g_a(t) + \psi_R g_b(t) + (1 - \hat t) u_b(x) - (1 - \hat t) \left(\psi_L g_a(t_0) + \psi_R g_b(t_0)\right)\]

at the physical time t = t_0 + h \hat t, with g_a and g_b the problem's bcs_function at the two ends and u_b the bottom datum. The field of the slab is P[N] + ℓ, so it is the data at the three parts of the boundary by construction: P[N] is zero there and ℓ is the datum.

The corner correction is the term ψ_L(x) * (1 - t̂) at the left end and ψ_R(x) * (1 - t̂) at the right. With it ℓ interpolates the sides at the slab's start and the bottom along the whole slab; without it the bottom would carry the sides' values at t_0 as well, and only the second slab, whose sides at t_0 are its predecessor's top, would show it.

The coupling carries no unknown of its own — no multiplier and no coefficient of its own — so coupling_unknowns is zero, coupling_term! adds nothing to the residual, and the slab hands the next one its top row alone. The interior weak form is the whole residual: there is no surface term and no load at the bottom of the slab.

The side values are the problem's boundary function and the bottom is its initial condition on the first slab and the previous slab's top afterwards, so the coupling reads no exact solution. A boundary function that does not meet the initial condition at a corner is a warning, once per integrator, and never an error.

A NetworkAnsatz and a TensorBSplineAnsatz are both accepted, and an ansatz of any other kind is refused when the integrator is built; the coefficient ansatz takes x_boundary = Dirichlet(). For a network the transform projects the field's derivative with respect to every free parameter together with the field, because the projection is linear in the parameters. A coefficient ansatz carries no parameter derivative, and its rows are its own basis functions rather than the projected ones: there the transform places the data in the field and the residual is a record of what the placement leaves.

source
MultisymplecticIntegrators.HardConstraintLiftingData — Type
HardConstraintLiftingData{ST, C} <: CouplingData

The block of the lifting: the coupling, the slab's start time start, the datum's u and its space derivative w at the nodes of the rule the residual reads, and one scratch vector work.

start is the physical time of the slab's bottom, so that a side value is read at start + h t̂, and it moves with the slab; the datum's two arrays move with it too.

work is laid out in four parts, the last empty where the ansatz carries no basis of its own:

  • the field of the two corners, (0, a) and (0, b), as [u; ∂_t̂u; ∂ₓu], 6D entries;
  • their derivative with respect to the nf free parameters, 6D · nf entries, entry (i, k) of the k-th parameter at 6D + (k - 1) · 6D + i;
  • the space nodes of the datum, one per row of bottom_u;
  • the traces of the basis of a coefficient ansatz at the three boundary parts: the space functions at the two ends of xspan, 2 · Nx entries, and the time functions at the slab's bottom, Nt entries.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.check_coupling — Method
check_coupling(problem, ::HardConstraintLifting, ansatz)

Refuse an ansatz the lifting cannot take, and a coefficient basis whose ends are not the data's.

The lifting's transform places the data in the field of a network slab and of a coefficient slab; a SymbolicAnsatz carries neither, so it is refused. A coefficient slab needs x_boundary = Dirichlet(): the transform reads the field of the slab's two ends at the time nodes of the residual, so a Free() space basis — free at the ends, and lifted there — would be lifted at the wrong time.

The corner check of the slab's data is not a refusal and is not here: the block's coupling_data reports it, once per integrator, as a warning.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.coupling_data — Method
coupling_data(c::HardConstraintLifting, ansatz, problem::LagrangianPDEProblem{T}, ST)

The lifting's block, built once for each cache that an integrator builds.

The cache of the problem's own element type T also reports a corner at which the problem's boundary function and its initial condition disagree, and the cache that a solver builds for another element type — a Dual on its first Jacobian — is built on top of that one and says nothing, so one integrator warns once.

The projection fixes the slab at the two ends in x and at its bottom, so the corners (0, a) and (0, b) carry two data. A problem whose boundary function is its exact solution meets its initial condition there exactly; a problem whose data disagree still runs, with the difference carried by the corner correction, and the warning names the corner and the difference so that such a run is visible in its log.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.transform_field! — Method
transform_field!(u, v, w, data, block::NetworkSlab, t̂, x, h, problem, Ju, Jv, Jw)

The lifting's transform of a network's field: the field, its two derivatives and — where Ju, Jv and Jw are this evaluation's — its derivative with respect to every free parameter become P[N] + ℓ at the nodes (t̂[q], x[r]).

The three parameter-derivative arrays default to the block's own, which are the ones the residual's evaluation filled. The core also transforms the top of the slab, at one time node and at another set of space points; that evaluation writes no parameter derivative, and the block's arrays are left alone there because their sizes are the residual's and not the top's.

The transform reads the ansatz's block and never the ansatz object, so it projects the parameters the step is solving for: block.values, block.gradient and the block's surfaces are one evaluation of block.params. It writes the field arrays alone, so the second call of a step — the one in update! — compounds nothing of the slab before it into the field.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.OrthogonalGreedy — Type
OrthogonalGreedy(; bias_interval = (-π, π), Nw = 50, Nb = 50) <: SlabFitMethod

The fit method of an ansatz whose parameters enter a nonlinearity, such as a NetworkAnsatz: a dictionary of σ(w₁ t̂ + w₂ x + b) atoms, from which the fit selects one atom per hidden unit and reads the output weights by least squares.

The dictionary is Nw + 1 directions of equal angle on the half-open circle [0, 2π), crossed with Nb + 1 biases over bias_interval. The fit is linear in the output weights alone, so the hidden weights and the biases stay where the dictionary put them; a Projection() is refused on such an ansatz, and this fit replaces it as the ansatz's default_fit.

        degree = 3), WeakInitialAndBoundaryConditions();

MultisymplecticIntegrators.PINNTraining — Type
PINNTraining(; epochs = 100, step = 1e-2, batch = 100, nsamples = 5_000,
             rng = Xoshiro(1234)) <: SlabFitMethod

The fit method of a NetworkAnsatz that trains every free parameter of the network against a sample of the slab's target, by plain gradient descent: epochs passes over the nsamples points of the slab's target, each pass cut into consecutive batches of batch points in their drawn order, with one step of the fixed step per batch on that batch's mean squared residual.

The points are drawn once per fit, uniformly over [0, 1] × xspan, from a copy of rng taken at the call and never from the process's global generator, so one method object gives every integrator that shares it the same points and the same fitted parameters. The target is evaluated at each of them by target_point!, so every target of the guess is read at the physical time of its point. The gradient is the derivative of the field with respect to each free parameter, which the ansatz's block already carries: the fit needs no differentiation and no optimiser package.

The descent writes the free parameters alone and leaves the parameters the mode holds fixed at their values, so a :last_layer network keeps its hidden layer. A fresh block's hidden layer is zero and no PINNTraining fit writes it, so on a :last_layer network of tanh units the field and its derivative with respect to every output weight stay zero, and the fit leaves the field at zero. Its one buffer, the batch gradient, is allocated once per call, before the epoch loop, and no epoch and no batch allocates.

method = SpaceTimeGalerkin(NetworkAnsatz(S = 10, xspan = (0.0, 1.0), ncells = (2, 5),
        degree = 3), WeakInitialAndBoundaryConditions();
    initial_guess = FitTo(InitialCondition(); method = PINNTraining(epochs = 100)))

MultisymplecticIntegrators.Projection — Type
Projection() <: SlabFitMethod

The fit method of an ansatz that is linear in its coefficients: the L² projection of the target onto the slab's ansatz space, on the quadrature of the slab.

The projection solves the mass-matrix system of the ansatz, which SimpleSplines factorises once per quadrature rather than once per step. An ansatz whose coefficients enter a nonlinearity has no such system, and a Projection() on one is refused at construction — see SpaceTimeGalerkin.

A user-defined fit method is a subtype of SlabFitMethod, and places the coefficients of coeffs, of size (Nt, Nx, D) for a tensor ansatz and the parameter vector of a network, from the sampled target, in one method of

fit!(coeffs, method, ansatz, sample, cache)

cache is the SpaceTimeGalerkinCache of the slab, which a fit may read; a projection keeps no scratch in it, and a network's block holds the workspace of its greedy fit.

source
MultisymplecticIntegrators.TrainedHiddenLayer — Type
TrainedHiddenLayer(; epochs = 100, learning_rate = 1e-5, rng = Xoshiro(1234)) <: SlabFitMethod

The fit method of a NetworkAnsatz in :last_layer mode that trains the hidden layer instead of taking it from a dictionary: every epoch takes one gradient-descent step on the hidden weights and the hidden biases, and then reads the output weights by least squares on the hidden features that step left.

The misfit the step descends is the field's distance from the sample of the slab, weighted as the slab's own measure weights it on the normalised time: the ansatz's interior rule by ωₜ ωₓ, the two surfaces at the space rule by ωₓ, and the two surfaces at the time rule by ωₜ. The features the solve reads are the hidden layer's activations at those same points, so the four surfaces carry the initial and boundary data into the fit.

epochs = 0 fits the output layer alone on the hidden layer the rng draws; a larger epochs descends first. The draw is taken from rng and never from the process's global generator, and the generator is copied per call, so one method object gives the same fit to every integrator that shares it. The buffers of the fit live in the ansatz's block, not in the method.

A network in :all mode is refused, because Newton determines the hidden layer there.

method = SpaceTimeGalerkin(NetworkAnsatz(S = 20, mode = :last_layer, xspan = (0.0, 1.0),
        ncells = (2, 5), degree = 3), WeakInitialAndBoundaryConditions();
    initial_guess = FitTo(InitialCondition(); method = TrainedHiddenLayer(epochs = 20)))

MultisymplecticIntegrators.TrainedFit — Type
TrainedFit{ST}

The workspace of a TrainedHiddenLayer fit in the block of a network ansatz: the sample the fit is taken on and the buffers of its two steps, which a fit of other sizes builds again.

points holds the normalised time and the space coordinate of every point of the sample, measure the weight the slab's measure gives that point, and target and values the target and the field there, one row per point and one column per component. features holds the hidden layer's activations at the points, weighted by the square root of measure, and rhs the target weighted the same way; features \ rhs is the least-squares problem whose solution is the output layer. gradient is the misfit's derivative with respect to the hidden parameters, and misfit the misfit of the fitted field.

The buffers are of the problem's element type. points and measure depend on the block's rules alone, and every fit overwrites the rest, so the method holds no state of the fit.
