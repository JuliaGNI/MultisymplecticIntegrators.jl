MultisymplecticIntegrators.FixedBoundaryNetwork — Type
FixedBoundaryNetwork(; Sx = 8, St = 10, bubble_form = :tsr, mode = :all, xspan,
                      nq = (6, 4), ncells = 20, D = 1)

The fixed-boundary network: the configuration of one time level is the parameter vector of a spatial network, φ = (w, b, c), and the slab between two levels carries a bubble Ψ whose hidden layer is St tanh units. There are no multipliers, and a step solves for the next level's parameters and the bubble's.

The spatial network is

\[N(x; \phi, t) = (1 - \hat s)\, g_a(t) + \hat s\, g_b(t) + (x - a)(b - x) \sum_i c_i \tanh(w_i \hat s + b_i), \qquad \hat s = \frac{x - a}{b - a},\]

with g_a and g_b the problem's side data at the two ends of the span. It carries the data of its own time level, so the slab's field can equal the data at every node of every step. The hidden layer serves all D components, Sx weights, Sx biases and Sx × D output weights, and one unit's argument is normalised in space, so the same network lives on any span.

bubble_form names the coordinates of the bubble's hidden layer: :abg is (α, β, γ), and :tsr is (θ, s, ρ) with (α, β, γ) = (e^ρ cos θ, e^ρ sin θ, −e^ρ s). mode fixes the unknowns of a step: :all frees the level's parameters, the bubble's hidden layer and its output weights, and :last_layer frees the level's output weights c and the bubble's output weights alone, with both hidden layers held as data.

The slab's own rule is nq[1] Gauss nodes in the normalised time and nq[2] Gauss nodes on each of ncells space cells, and it is not the problem's output grid.

ansatz = FixedBoundaryNetwork(Sx = 8, St = 10, xspan = (0.0, 1.0), ncells = 20)

MultisymplecticIntegrators.FixedBoundarySlab — Type
FixedBoundarySlab{ST, T, A}

The block of a FixedBoundaryNetwork in the slab cache: what the ansatz keeps of one slab.

params is the whole parameter vector of the slab — the solved level's parameters, then the bubble's hidden block, then its output weights — and residual the action's gradient on the equations of the step: the derivative with respect to the bottom level's free parameters and to the bubble's, one row per unknown. momentum holds the third block of that gradient, the derivative with respect to the solved level's free parameters, which is the momentum the step hands on.

u, v and w hold the field and its derivatives at the nodes of the slab's own rule, Ju, Jv and Jw their derivative with respect to each equation's parameter, and Jt_u, Jt_v and Jt_w their derivative with respect to each of the solved level's free parameters. datum is what the block keeps of the coupling's datum, which the evaluation at the top of the slab needs; top_sample is the target at t̂ = 1 on the space nodes, which the fit of the level reads. fit is the workspace of the bubble's fit.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.NetworkAnsatz — Type
NetworkAnsatz(; S, activation = tanh, mode = :all, xspan, ncells, degree,
                quadrature_order = nothing, D = 1)

A neural-network ansatz of one space-time slab: a two-layer network whose input is the normalised time t̂ and the physical space coordinate x, and whose output is the D components of the field. NetworkAnsatz <: SpaceTimeAnsatz, so a SpaceTimeGalerkin holds it as its ansatz.

The network is

\[u_d(\hat t, x) = \sum_{s=1}^{S} W^{(2)}_{ds} \, \sigma\bigl(W^{(1)}_{s1} \hat t + W^{(1)}_{s2} x + b_s\bigr),\]

that is Chain(Dense(2, S, activation), Dense(S, D, identity; use_bias = false)). Its parameters are the hidden weights (S, 2), the hidden biases (S,) and the output weights (D, S), in the order L1.W, L1.b, L2.W of NeuralNetworkParameters, and one hidden layer serves all D components.

mode fixes which parameters the solver determines: :all frees every one of them, and :last_layer frees the output weights alone and leaves the hidden layer where the initial guess put it, so that the step is affine in its unknowns, and two Newton iterations reach its tolerance wherever the output weights carry every constraint row of the coupling. The parameters live in the ansatz's block in the slab cache and never in the ansatz, so two integrators that share one ansatz object give identical results.

The ansatz carries a TensorBSplineAnsatz as its trace basis, trace_basis(ansatz), built from degree and ncells on the same xspan with Free() at both ends, the condition the weak initial-and-boundary coupling needs. The functions of that basis are the multipliers of a coupling that imposes the slab's data weakly, and its rule is the one the coupling's tables and its surface rows are built on. The field itself is evaluated on a rule of the ansatz's own: quadrature_order Gauss nodes per cell in the time direction, and the space grid of the trace basis, which the cache's trace is kept on. nothing takes one node per cell more than the trace basis has, so that the two rules are not the same one.

An xspan that does not hold two values, an S or a D that is not positive, and a mode that is neither :all nor :last_layer raise ArgumentError. An activation that the symbolic derivatives of SymbolicNeuralNetworks cannot reach raises that package's own error, which is not caught here.

ansatz = NetworkAnsatz(S = 10, xspan = (0.0, 1.0), ncells = (2, 5), degree = 3)

MultisymplecticIntegrators.NetworkSlab — Type
NetworkSlab{ST, T, A}

The block of a NetworkAnsatz in the slab cache: what the ansatz keeps of one slab.

params is the network's parameter vector of the slab, in the order L1.W, L1.b, L2.W, and residual its interior weak form, one row per free parameter. u, v and w hold the field and its derivatives at the nodes of the ansatz's own rule, and Ju, Jv and Jw their derivative with respect to each free parameter there. bottom, top, left and right hold the field and its parameter derivative at the four surfaces of the slab, on the trace basis' rules, which is where the coupling's rows read them. tabulation is the ansatz's own rule and trace_tabulation the trace basis', which the coupling's tables are built on; output and trace are the two point sets the core evaluates the top of the slab at, and fit the workspace of the greedy fit.

values and gradient are the scratch of one evaluation — the 3D values of the field, its two derivatives and their parameter derivative — input its two-entry argument, and parameter_set the parameters laid out as the network's kernels read them, which every evaluation refreshes from params. train is the workspace of the TrainedHiddenLayer fit, which trains the hidden layer in place.

GeometricIntegratorsBase.initsolver — Method
initsolver(solvermethod, method, caches)

The network family solves its slab with a rank-revealing linear solve.

A network's rows are its parameters, and the constraint rows of a multiplier coupling depend on the field alone, so the Jacobian is a saddle point whose rank is at most the number of parameters plus the number of constraints, and the constraint block reaches only the output weights in :last_layer mode. The minimum-norm step of a complete orthogonal factorization solves a consistent system exactly and drops the directions the rank does not carry, where a factorisation without rank detection loses the descent direction and raises on the zero pivot.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.bubble_chart — Function
bubble_chart(α, β, γ, unit = 0)

The (θ, s, ρ) coordinates of a hidden unit whose coordinates are (α, β, γ): ρ = log √(α² + β²), θ = atan(β, α) and s = −γ / e^ρ.

A unit at α = β = 0, that is r = 0, has no (θ, s, ρ) coordinates — the radius is the exponential of ρ, which is never zero — so converting one raises ArgumentError naming the unit instead of returning a NaN.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.bubble_derivative — Method
bubble_derivative(ansatz, Θ, i, d, t̂, ŝ)

The bubble's sum at one point, its two derivatives, and their derivative with respect to the free parameter i of Θ: a hidden entry, in the coordinates of the ansatz's form, or an output weight.

The hidden entries' derivatives are taken in (α, β, γ) and carried to the form's coordinates by the chart's own Jacobian, so that a :tsr residual is the gradient of the action written in (θ, s, ρ) and its derivative is the chart applied to the equations as well as to the unknowns. An output weight contributes to the one component it belongs to, which its position in Θ names: its derivative in another component is zero.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.fit! — Method
fit!(w, method::OrthogonalGreedy, ansatz::NetworkAnsatz, sample, cache)

Fit the network to a slab's sampled target, by the greedy fit of fit_network! on the workspace of the cache's block, and write the fitted atoms into w in the order L1.W, L1.b, L2.W: the two hidden weights and the bias of every selected atom, and the output weight of every component behind it. A unit the selection passed over leaves its parameters at zero.

The output weights are the ones the fit returns: the projection is orthogonal to the atoms already taken, so its coefficients are those of the atoms and not of the network, and the fit's triangular solve turns them into the output weights of a hidden layer that is already fixed.

source
MultisymplecticIntegrators.fit! — Method
fit!(w, method::PINNTraining, ansatz::NetworkAnsatz, sample, cache)

Fit the network to a slab's sampled target by epochs passes over the sample's points, one gradient-descent step of the method's fixed step per batch of batch consecutive points, on that batch's mean squared residual of the field against the sample's values. The points keep the order they were drawn in, and the last batch, which is the shorter one, is averaged over its own size. sample is the named tuple (; points, values, start) that fit_sample returns for the method.

The fit draws no point and evaluates no target: both are the sample's. It takes the sample's start where the free entries of w are all zero — a tanh network is stationary at the zero parameters every block begins with — and the parameters it is given anywhere else, so a :last_layer fit leaves the block's hidden layer untouched either way. The gradient is the derivative of the field with respect to each free parameter, which the block's gradient holds at every point it evaluates, so the descent needs no differentiation of its own. The batch gradient is allocated once, before the epoch loop, and each point is evaluated into the block's own scratch, so no epoch and no batch allocates.

The free parameters alone are written, and they are written into the block as well as into w, so that the evaluation of every point reads the parameters the descent has reached; a parameter the mode holds fixed keeps its value.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.fit! — Method
fit!(w, method::TrainedHiddenLayer, ansatz::NetworkAnsatz, sample, cache)

MultisymplecticIntegrators.fit_sample — Method
fit_sample(::PINNTraining, interior, target, cache, ansatz, problem, sol)

The sample of a PINNTraining fit: nsamples points of the slab drawn uniformly over [0, 1] × xspan from a copy of the method's generator, and the value of the target at each of them, read by target_point! so that every target of the guess answers at the physical time of its point. The points are drawn afresh at every call and the method's own generator is never advanced, so two fits that share one method object draw the same points. Each coordinate is one Float64 draw rounded to the element type, so a fit in Float32 reads the points of the same fit in Float64.

Beside them the sample carries start: one value in [-1, 1] per free parameter, drawn from the same copy of the generator after the points. A network whose parameters are all zero has a zero derivative with respect to every one of them, so fit! starts from start there and from the parameters it is given anywhere else.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.side_derivative — Method
side_derivative(f, t)

The derivative of a scalar side function of time at t, by ForwardDiff on the function itself.

The lift of a slab differentiates the problem's side data in time, and the data function is the only source of that derivative: a bcs_function that is not generic in its time argument therefore raises where the integrator is built.

ForwardDiff marks neither derivative nor gradient public, and this module binds the name derivative to SymbolicNeuralNetworks' already, so its own derivative is imported from neither: the scalar derivative is the gradient of the same function of one variable.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.slab_component — Method
slab_component(ansatz, d, φ0, φ1, Θ, t̂, x, h, sides)

One component of the slab's field at one point, and its two derivatives: u, ∂_{t̂}u and ∂ₓu, from the two levels' parameters, the bubble's, and the side data sides of that point.

The field is the two levels' networks weighted by 1 − t̂ and t̂, the lift G that carries the side data of the slab's own time, and the bubble whose two weights vanish at both ends of the interface, so that the field equals the side data at x = a and x = b for every parameter vector.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.slab_field! — Method
slab_field!(u, v, w, slab, x, bottom, nf, h)

The field of the slab that the solver's vector x describes, at the nodes of the slab's own rule: the free parameters from x, the levels' and the bubble's fixed parameters from the block and the coupling's datum, and the field, its derivatives and their parameter derivative by the network of the two levels and the bubble. v is the derivative in physical time, and the parameter derivative of v carries the same 1 / h.

The parameter derivative of the field is that of an equation's own parameter: the bottom level's for the first rows, whose equations the datum's momentum is added to, and the bubble's behind them. The bottom level's parameters are data of the step, so its derivative is taken in the block's arrays rather than in the solver's vector, and the solved level's derivative goes to the momentum block.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.SymbolicAnsatz — Type
SymbolicAnsatz(expr, t, x, p; xspan, start, degree = 1, ncells = (2, 2), n = nothing)

A symbolic ansatz of one space-time slab whose field is a closed-form expression.

The slab is the rectangle [0, 1] × xspan in the normalised time coordinate t̂ and the physical space coordinate x, and the field is

\[u(t̂, x) = \mathrm{expr}(t̂, x, p),\]

where expr is a Num or a Vector{Num} of D entries, p the vector of its parameter symbols, and start the initial guess of the parameter vector on the first slab. The field's time is the slab's normalised time, so an expression in physical time is substituted before it is given here.

The constructor builds six in-place functions with Symbolics.build_function(…; expression = Val(false)): the field, its two derivatives ∂u/∂t̂ and ∂u/∂x, and the parameter Jacobian of each, a D × length(p) matrix. They are held in concrete fields, so no container of abstract type and no eval of generated code enters a step; each function compiles once, at its first call. The ansatz also holds a TensorBSplineAnsatz named trace, whose rule is the slab's quadrature and whose space basis carries the coupling's multiplier tables; the trace basis does not constrain the field.

The constructor refuses an expression whose free symbols are not t, x and the entries of p, an empty p, a start of another length than p and an xspan that does not increase, each with an ArgumentError naming the offending value. check_ansatz refuses an expression count that is not the problem's D, an element type that is not the problem's, and an xspan that is not the problem's.

The parameters are the unknowns of the slab. WeakInitialAndBoundaryConditions is the one coupling the ansatz takes, and it lets the field be free at both ends of xspan, so the trace basis is built with the Free() boundary condition that coupling needs.

The integrator stores the solved top multipliers as the top_multiplier entry of each step's internal state, as it does for a spline ansatz. For a symbolic ansatz they are not unique: the Jacobian of a step has a null space in the top and side multipliers that leaves the parameters determined, so the stored values carry no meaning on their own.

SymbolicAnsatz(p[1] * sin(p[2] * x - p[3] * t), t, x, p;
    xspan = (0, 2π), start = [1, 1, 2π])

params is the parameter vector of the slab and residual its interior weak form, of one row per parameter. u, v and w hold the field and its derivatives at the nodes of the slab's quadrature, and Ju, Jv and Jw the parameter Jacobian of each there. bottom, top, left and right hold the field and its parameter Jacobian at the four boundary sets: t̂ = 0 and t̂ = 1 at the space nodes, and x = a and x = b at the time nodes. tabulation holds the tables of the slab's quadrature, and output and trace evaluate the trace basis' space basis at the output grid and at the nodes of the space rule. GeometricIntegratorsBase.initsolver — Method
initsolver(solvermethod, method, caches)

The symbolic family solves its slab with a rank-revealing linear solve.

The multiplier coupling's Jacobian is a saddle point whose constraint rows depend only on the field, so its rank is at most twice the parameter count: for the three-parameter travelling wave p₁ sin(p₂x − p₃t̂) on a trace basis of degree one and two cells, the first slab's 10 × 10 matrix has rank 6, and its four smallest singular values are at round-off. The minimum-norm step of a complete orthogonal factorization solves a consistent system exactly and drops the directions the rank does not carry, where a factorisation without rank detection loses the descent direction: started at its exact parameters, that run takes three to 29 iterations per slab with the ecosystem's LU and reaches the tolerance on its first slab alone in Float64, where the pivoted QR converges in two iterations at every slab in both precisions.

MultisymplecticIntegrators.TensorBSplineAnsatz — Type
TensorBSplineAnsatz(; degree, ncells, xspan, x_boundary = Dirichlet(), n = nothing)

A tensor B-spline ansatz of one space-time slab, on SimpleSplines.

The slab is the rectangle [0, 1] × xspan in the normalised time coordinate t̂ and the physical space coordinate x. The ansatz approximates the field by

\[u_h(t̂, x) = \sum_{i,j} \hat{u}_{ij} \, B_i(t̂) \, B_j(x),\]

with a clamped B-spline basis of degree degree[1] on ncells[1] uniform cells of [0, 1] and a B-spline basis of degree degree[2] on ncells[2] uniform cells of xspan. degree and ncells are given per direction, as a number for both or as a (time, space) tuple, so that the two directions may differ in degree and in cell count.

x_boundary is the boundary condition the space basis is built with: Dirichlet() recombines the basis at both ends of xspan, so that every function vanishes there, and Free() keeps the natural condition. The ansatz carries a homogeneous Dirichlet value in x only; an inhomogeneous value is a coupling's business.

n is the number of Gauss–Legendre nodes per cell in each direction, again a number or a (time, space) tuple; the default integrates the products of the basis functions and of their first derivatives exactly, which is p + 1 nodes for degree p, the count the products of the basis functions need.

Both degrees are at least 1. The slab core reads the space gradient ∂ₓu from the same space basis, and a piecewise-constant degree-0 basis carries none, so a lower degree raises ArgumentError.

The ansatz counts the degree, which is what SimpleSplines takes; a basis of order k is one of degree k - 1.

ansatz = TensorBSplineAnsatz(degree = 2, ncells = (1, 20), xspan = (0.0, 1.0))
