The B-spline family

A SpaceTimeGalerkin method with a TensorBSplineAnsatz discretises the field of one slab by a tensor product of B-splines in the normalised time t̂ and in x. Three couplings join consecutive slabs:

couplingthe slab's datathe space basis belowat 294f023
CoefficientRestrictionthe bottom coefficient row is fixed, the momentum flux is carriedDirichlet()Galerkin_Full_Restriction_Bspline_Integrator, SpaceTime_Spline_Wave_Integrator, FEM_Multisymplectic_Integrator
WeakInitialConditionthe initial condition by multipliersDirichlet()Galerkin_Dirichlet_Bspline_Integrator
WeakInitialAndBoundaryConditionsthe initial and the boundary conditions by multipliersFree()Galerkin_Bspline_Integrator

At degree 1 under CoefficientRestriction the step is the bilinear finite-element scheme of the old FEM integrator. The step is a one-step form, so it needs no start-up, and its velocity is the ansatz's own ∂u/∂t̂ / h. The space basis' boundary condition is SimpleSplines' Dirichlet() or Free(), and SimpleSplines counts the degree of a basis, not its order.

The three couplings

Every example on this page integrates Wave for two steps of h = 0.1 on twenty space cells.

using MultisymplecticIntegrators
using SimpleSplines: Dirichlet, Free

problem = Wave.problem(timespan = (0.0, 0.2), timestep = 0.1, xstep = 0.05)
ansatz = TensorBSplineAnsatz(degree = 2, ncells = (1, 20), xspan = (0.0, 1.0),
    x_boundary = Dirichlet())
result = integrate(problem, SpaceTimeGalerkin(ansatz, CoefficientRestriction()))
result.stats
(iterations = [1, 1], residuals = [1.8941566771170386e-16, 2.8248535508638434e-16], initial_residuals = [0.15675298679328928, 0.10749604979617558], converged = Bool[1, 1])
result = integrate(problem, SpaceTimeGalerkin(ansatz, WeakInitialCondition()))
result.stats
(iterations = [1, 1], residuals = [3.2695982347709976e-16, 3.9598162639021054e-16], initial_residuals = [0.15752890227845132, 0.10879627460568095], converged = Bool[1, 1])
free = TensorBSplineAnsatz(degree = 2, ncells = (1, 20), xspan = (0.0, 1.0),
    x_boundary = Free())
result = integrate(problem, SpaceTimeGalerkin(free, WeakInitialAndBoundaryConditions()))
result.stats
(iterations = [1, 1], residuals = [6.180813592810113e-16, 4.4035718549883583e-16], initial_residuals = [0.15753067216301936, 0.10879789652246244], converged = Bool[1, 1])

Both weak couplings take the keyword multiplier_basis: :bspline, the ansatz's own trace basis, the default, or :lagrange, the nodal basis of the same set of functions.

result = integrate(problem,
    SpaceTimeGalerkin(ansatz, WeakInitialCondition(multiplier_basis = :lagrange)))
result.stats
(iterations = [1, 1], residuals = [3.00895080048169e-16, 2.2223035089118977e-16], initial_residuals = [0.15752890227845134, 0.10879627460568088], converged = Bool[1, 1])

result is the named tuple (; solution, traces, stats) of integrate: the GeometricSolution on the output grid, the top trace of every step, and the record of every step's Newton solve.

The lifting on a Dirichlet ansatz

HardConstraintLifting, the coupling of the TrialNN family, also takes a B-spline ansatz whose space basis is Dirichlet(). The slab's field is P[N] + ℓ: the ansatz's field N projected so that it vanishes at both ends of xspan and at the bottom of the slab, plus the transfinite interpolant ℓ of the slab's data. A Dirichlet basis makes N zero at the two ends, but not at the bottom, so the projection still subtracts the bottom trace of N. It samples that trace at the space nodes of the ansatz's quadrature rule and subtracts the linear blend of the samples between two nodes, so P[N] vanishes on the bottom at those nodes, not between them. The bottom datum is stored at the same nodes, as the TrialNN page describes. No momentum is carried. The call below does not converge on this page's problem: the slab's Jacobian is singular to round-off, as K77 of KNOWN_ISSUES.md records, so the call is shown and not run. The lifting's tests record this arm's solves and do not assert that they converge.

result = integrate(problem, SpaceTimeGalerkin(ansatz, HardConstraintLifting()))

The direct linear solve

For an ansatz whose step is affine in its unknowns and a linear density, such as Wave's, linear_solver = true replaces Newton's iterations by two solves per step with one factorisation of the slab's Jacobian, taken at the run's first step.

result = integrate(problem,
    SpaceTimeGalerkin(ansatz, CoefficientRestriction(); linear_solver = true))
result.stats
(iterations = [2, 2], residuals = [1.817185069903448e-16, 1.9968266127170463e-16], initial_residuals = [0.15675298679328925, 0.1074960497961756], converged = Bool[1, 1])

The stability bound

The B-spline slab integrator on the wave equation is stable only up to about c·h/Δx ≤ 1, with c the wave speed, h the time step and Δx the width of a space cell. Above the bound the high space modes grow from round-off, while every step still converges, so a short run looks right. At degree 1 the step is the Newmark scheme with β = 1/6 and γ = 1/2. The examples above run Wave (c = 0.5) at h = 0.1 and Δx = 0.05, which is c·h/Δx = 1, at the bound. The measurements are in K62 of KNOWN_ISSUES.md; the package has no stable time rule.
